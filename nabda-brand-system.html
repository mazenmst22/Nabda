§00 — Strategy

Before the logo, the argument

Nabda is not competing on features. In Egypt and the Levant, healthcare booking is won on one thing: does this feel like it will actually work? Every design decision below is downstream of that question.

The tension we are designing against

Finding a doctor here still runs on WhatsApp screenshots, a receptionist who may or may not pick up, and a neighbour's recommendation. People are not short of options — they are short of certainty. The category's default look (stock photo of a smiling doctor, corporate blue, English-first) signals foreign, expensive, probably not for me.

So the brand's job is not to look advanced. It is to look certain — and then be surprisingly intelligent once you're inside.

Brand idea

One beat. Then everything follows.

A pulse is the smallest possible proof that a system is alive. Nabda's whole promise compresses into that: one tap, one beat, and the machinery moves — the doctor is found, the slot is held, the reminder is sent, Pulse is awake at 2am.

Design consequence: the identity is built from a single beat, never a busy waveform. Restraint is the message.

Positioning

For Arabic-speaking patients who need care they can trust and afford, Nabda is the healthcare platform that turns finding, booking and remembering a doctor's visit into a single conversation — because Pulse does the work you'd otherwise do by phone.

Personality — the dial

Calm over energetic · plain over clever · warm over clinical · certain over aspirational · quietly modern over futuristic. Nabda is the composed nurse at the desk who already has your file open, not the startup founder on stage.

What we refuse

No stethoscope-around-neck stock photography. No corporate cobalt blue. No English-first hierarchy. No AI sparkles. No urgency theatre — health anxiety is not a growth lever.

The two registers — this is the whole system in one line

Register A — The Platform

Faience teal, deep ink, generous white space, zero motion beyond a fade. This is where booking happens, and booking must feel like paperwork that went right. Teal never glows.

Register B — Pulse, the intelligence layer

Gold. Luminous, breathing, always the only thing on screen that emits light. Gold is reserved exclusively for Pulse — if it glows, it is thinking. Users learn this in one session without being told.

This gold-means-intelligence rule is the single most valuable asset in this document. Protect it. The moment gold is used for a promo banner or a "Book now" button, the system loses its only piece of learned meaning.

§01 — Logo system

Five marks, five arguments

Each concept is a different answer to "what is Nabda, in one shape?" They are not variations — they are competing theories. One is recommended; the others are live options with honest trade-offs stated.

§02 — Visual identity

Construction, clear space, and the one motif

The identity has exactly one repeating device beyond the logo. Systems fail when they have five. Everything below governs the recommended mark, 01 Nuqta.

Construction — everything derives from X

The gold dot's diameter is the module. Bowl stroke = 0.76X. Bowl width = 5.9X. Cap height of the wordmark = 2.1X. Wordmark sits at 1X from the mark. Nothing in the identity is measured in pixels — only in X.

Below 24px the beat is dropped and the mark reduces to the bowl and the dot — the pure ن. The heartbeat is carried by the interval motif instead. Never shrink the full mark past the point where the spike closes up; a muddy logo is worse than a simplified one.

The one motif — "the interval"

A long flat line with one beat, never a repeating waveform. It runs as a hairline divider between sections, along the bottom of the app header, and across the top of email templates. The flatness is the point — calm, interrupted once. A repeating ECG reads as an emergency room.

Misuse — each of these has been seen in a real health-tech brand

Never stretch or condense. Scale proportionally only.

Never recolour outside the palette. Gold is Pulse; nothing else glows.

No shadows, bevels, outlines or 3D. The mark is flat, always.

Never rotate. The bowl must sit level or the ن stops being a ن.

Never place on a busy or off-palette background. Use ink, paper or teal.

§05 — Brand guidelines

How Nabda behaves

Voice, imagery, motion and the rules that stop a brand system from eroding in month four.

Voice — four settings

Plain, never simplified

Short sentences, ordinary words, full respect. "Your appointment is confirmed for Tuesday at 9:30." Not "Awesome! You're all set 🎉".

Certain, never boastful

State what will happen. "We'll remind you the night before and an hour ahead." Nabda earns trust by being specific, not by claiming to be trusted.

Warm, never familiar

Address people the way a good receptionist does — kind, professional, not your friend. In Arabic use حضرتك, not the casual second person.

Calm under bad news

Cancellations, no-shows, unavailable doctors: no exclamation marks, no apology theatre. Say what happened, then say the next step.

Photography direction

Real Egyptian and Levantine clinics, real faces, ordinary daylight. Grandmothers, working fathers, women in hijab and women without, a teenager on a phone in a waiting room. Nabda's users are not a lifestyle segment.

  • ✓Available light from a window; slightly cool, unfiltered
  • ✓Hands, phones and paperwork — the texture of the real task
  • ✓Eye-level framing; the subject is doing something, not posing
  • ✕Stethoscope draped over a shoulder. White-coat-arms-crossed. Anyone smiling at the camera in a lab coat.
  • ✕Blue-tinted "technology" imagery, floating holograms, DNA helices
  • ✕Distress, needles, hospital beds, anything that raises the reader's pulse
Iconography & motion

Icons: 24px grid, 1.75px stroke, round caps and joins, no fills. Drawn from the same geometry as the mark — same corner radii, same terminal treatment. Outline only; a filled icon in this system means "active", nothing else.

Motion, Register A: 160–220ms, cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1), opacity and 4–8px translate only. No bounce, no spring, no scale-in. Booking flows must feel like a door closing properly, not a toy.

Motion, Register B (Pulse only): continuous, breathing, 1.9–2.4s cycles. Pulse is the only element in the product allowed to animate while idle. Everything honours prefers-reduced-motion: the breathing stops, the gold stays.

Accessibility — non-negotiable in a health product

Contrast

4.5:1 minimum for all body text, 3:1 for UI boundaries. The verified table in §03 is the source of truth — re-run it before any palette change ships.

Targets & text

44×44px minimum touch targets. Body text never below 16px — many users are older and reading on a cracked screen. Support 200% zoom without horizontal scroll.

Never colour alone

Availability, confirmation and cancellation each carry a shape and a word as well as a colour. Roughly 1 in 12 men in the region has a colour vision deficiency.

§06 — Pulse

The avatar is a light, not a face

Pulse is an assistant inside a health product. The moment it gets eyes, a name badge or a cartoon body, it starts making promises about companionship it cannot keep — and in healthcare that is a liability, not a delight. Pulse is represented as a single point of warm light that behaves. Its behaviour is the personality.

Concept A — "Nuqta Bloom" · recommended · this is the gold dot from the logo, alive
Idle · awake
Listening
Thinking
Speaking

The same object in four behaviours. Idle breathes at roughly 25 breaths per minute — slower than a resting heart rate, because the job is to calm the room. Listening sends ripples outward (it is receiving). Thinking rotates a single arc — no dots, no spinner, no "AI is generating". Speaking becomes a five-bar waveform, which is also the voice-note affordance every WhatsApp user in the region already understands.

Concept B — "Aperture"

Two counter-rotating arc pairs around a fixed core. Reads as a lens focusing — apt for "finding you the right doctor". More mechanical than A, and it costs more attention. Good as the search / matching state inside concept A rather than as a separate identity.

Concept C — "The Interval"

No orb at all — Pulse is the brand's interval motif, drawn in gold, animating left to right as it thinks. The most restrained option and the most defensibly on-brand, but it has no resting state and is hard to place as a 32px chat avatar. Best used as the Pulse loading bar, not the avatar.

The handoff state is the ethical one. When a real doctor or clinic staff member joins the conversation, the gold must go. Users have to be able to tell, at a glance and without reading, whether they are talking to software or a person. Never blur that line — not for engagement, not for polish.

Pulse conversational rules
  • ✓Opens in the user's language, detected from their first message — not from a menu
  • ✓Names its limits early: "I can book, move or cancel appointments. I can't give medical advice."
  • ✓Confirms before acting: states doctor, date, time and price, then waits
  • ✓Handles Egyptian colloquial Arabic, not just Modern Standard
  • ✕Never diagnoses, never suggests a medication, never estimates severity
  • ✕Never uses emoji, exclamation marks, or "I'm so sorry to hear that"
  • ✕Never claims to be human, and never dodges the question when asked
  • ✕On any emergency signal, stops assisting and surfaces emergency numbers immediately

§08 — Website & product

Design direction and the component seed

Nabda's interface should feel like a well-run reception desk: everything already prepared, nothing shouting. Below is the token layer and the first eight components, specified tightly enough to build from.

Six layout principles

1 — Availability is the hero, not the doctor

Every doctor card leads with the next open slot. The photo is secondary. People are not shopping for a personality, they are solving Tuesday.

2 — One primary action per screen

Exactly one teal filled button in view at a time. Everything else is secondary or ghost.

3 — Never a blank state

No results always offers Pulse: "No cardiologist in Maadi before Thursday. Ask Pulse to widen the search?"

4 — Prices are always visible

Hidden pricing is the category's original sin and the number one reason people abandon. Show EGP before the booking step, always.

5 — Mobile is not the small version

Design 390px first. ~85% of this market books on a mid-range Android over a patchy connection. Budget 120KB of critical CSS+JS, ship system-font fallbacks.

6 — RTL is the default build

Use logical properties throughout (margin-inline-start, not margin-left). LTR is the mirrored case, not the base case.
